## 8. CAPTION

Most packs use the word "organic" like a design element. It sits above the ingredient line, next to a green leaf, and nobody stops to ask what it's actually promising.

Here are the 5 questions that separate a real organic pack from a hopeful one.

One — does the pack carry a proper certification mark, not just the word itself? Look for India Organic, Jaivik Bharat, PGS-India, or USDA Organic printed on the front.

Two — is the certifier named, with an accreditation number you can look up? A real cert has a body and a code. "Organic" alone is decoration.

Three — is the FSSAI licence number printed on the pack? Every packaged food in India needs one, and if it isn't there, that pack shouldn't be on the shelf.

Four — does the ingredient list use the word organic on the ingredient itself? "Organic biscuits" doesn't mean the flour, sugar, and oil inside are organic. Read line by line.

Five — can you trace it back? A real organic brand tells you who grew it, where it was milled, and where it was packed. Batch number, date of packaging, factory address — all of it printed on the pack.

The Health Fields Organic Bajra Atta 500g on our shelf passes all five. Same pack answers every question on this list. If it pairs well, the Health Fields Ragi Atta on the same shelf follows the exact same disclosure discipline.

## 11. VARIATION

The word organic on the front of a pack isn't a certification. It's a font choice.

The five things that separate a real organic pack from marketing.

A certification mark on the front. India Organic, Jaivik Bharat, PGS-India, or USDA — not just the word "organic" sitting above the ingredient line.

A certifier named on the back, with a code. If nobody's willing to put their accreditation number on your pack, nobody's really behind the claim.

An FSSAI licence number printed clearly. Non-negotiable for any packaged food sold in India.

An ingredient list where "organic" sits on the ingredient itself. Not on the brand name, not on the product name — on the flour, the oil, the dal.

A traceable back panel. Manufacturer, factory address, batch, date of packaging. If a pack can't tell you where it was made, it probably shouldn't be feeding your kids.
